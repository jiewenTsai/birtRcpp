# birtRcpp 0.1.0

* `score_test(fit, add = Y ~ delta[item] * z)`: Lagrange multiplier (score) tests for declared models, from the `ecm()` (or `mml()`) fit of the null model only (Rao, 1948; for IRT Glas, 1998, 1999; Glas & van der Linden, 2010). `add` names an observed part and a term added to its linear predictor (`bernoulli_logit()`) or mean (`normal()`); new parameters are written with their dimension (`delta[item]`) or as one value, and are 0 under the null. Covariates come from the data of `block_model()` or from `data = list(z = )`. The score is the expected complete-data score of the E-step (Fisher's identity) and the information is the observed information by Louis' formula, with the null parameters projected out; one joint test and one test per item (Holm, with the one-step estimate). Directions that the null parameters absorb (e.g. a shift of every item with a free latent mean) are found from the casewise scores and left out of the joint test. Checks (2PL, 500 persons, 8 items, 100 replications): LM and LR statistics correlate 0.9995 without DIF and 0.997-0.999 with DIF; size under the null 5.0% (joint, KS p = 0.62) and 4.7% per item (KS p = 0.38), also with impact and a latent mean (4.0%, 5.2%; df 7); power for a uniform DIF of 0.5 / 0.8 logits on one item 57% / 92%. It reproduces `ci_test()` of the built-in cross model (`add = logT ~ delta[item] * Y`; statistics within 4e-4) and the Merkle-Zeileis LM test of `score_test()` for b along a binary moderator (correlation 1.000).
* Louis' information for declared models with person-level parameters that are not integrated out (`ecm(latent = "theta")` with a fixed speed per person) is analytic: the covariance of the complete-data scores is block diagonal by person and is computed person by person. Standard errors equal the numerical derivative of the score to 3e-8 (relative); the fit of 200 persons x 6 items takes 0.3 s instead of 1.5 s, and its Hessian 0.01 s instead of 1.5 s. Estimates and standard errors of the other fits are unchanged (identical).
* `algorithm()` of declared Gibbs (`gibbs()`, `algorithm(model, steps = )`) and ECM fits is written as formulas, as for `vi()`: the model p, then each step as its full conditional (Gibbs: `aᵢ | · ∼ N⁺(mᵢ/pᵢ, 1/pᵢ)` with p and m written out, Pólya-Gamma and half-t auxiliary steps, MALA, shifts, cuts) or, for ECM, the objective, the E-step (quadrature, exact integration, E[omega]) and each conditional mode with its expectations; `mml()` fits show one maximization. Unicode or `format = "latex"`. Expressions are printed with the parentheses they need (x − (a − b); the residuals of the CAVI variance updates were printed without them).
* `vi(quadrature = "ba81")`: common nodes with the Bock-Aitkin collapse of the logistic parts, as in `ecm()`. When a latent variable is integrated exactly and its parts are affine in the latent variables (speed in the RT models), `vi()` now uses the steps of `ecm()`: the exact step from a quadratic per person instead of at every node, and each person's mean and covariance through 2L points in the updates. Same fixed point as before (1e-13), the ELBO never decreases (`check_vi(quadrature = )`). 1000 persons x 20 items (cross RT model): 203 s before, 56 s now, 21 s with `quadrature = "ba81"` (same 771 sweeps and ELBO; ba81 within 6e-4; machine load 1.7-2.9 for all three).
* First CRAN release. ECM is the main estimator: `ecm()` (Meng & Rubin, 1993; was `em()`, which was ECM all along), with `check_ecm()` and `gibbs(init = "ecm")`; direct maximization by Newton or quasi-Newton steps is `mml()` (was `em(method = "newton")`). Declared models (`block_model()`) are fitted with the same verbs as the built-in models: `ecm()`, `gibbs()` and `vi()` (variational Bayes); `ecm()` takes `nodes` and `prior = "ml" | "map"` for both; `gibbs()` takes one set of argument names (`n_iter`, `n_chain`, `n_burnin`, `n_thin`; Julia's `cov2one` only in `sampleGibbs()`). Samplers written by the user are checked with `check_sampler(step = )`. `draw_shift(lower = )` draws a shift under truncated priors exactly (replaces `draw_location_shift()`). DIF trees moved to birt (`birt::dif_tree()`).
* The built-in models port the Julia package ExtendedRtIrtModeling.jl with corrected full conditionals.
* Faster ECM for declared models (same maxima): starting values from the data for intercept-like parameters (e.g. lambda at the mean log time, b at the logit of the proportion correct; `gibbs()`, `ecm()`, `mml()`; `vi()` keeps the prior means so that the ELBO rises from the first sweep), one Polya-Gamma expectation per M-step shared by the parameters of a logistic part, and quadrature cells of negligible weight skipped. On 1000 persons x 20 items (cross RT model, no starting values) `ecm()` went from 44 s (182 iterations) to 5-7 s, below `mml()` (Newton, 16-20 s). The default `tol` of `ecm()` for declared models is now 1e-10, as for the built-in models. Printing and `fit_indices()` of ECM fits say "ECM"; `prior = "map"` reports no AIC / BIC; `convergence()`, `compare_para()` and `plot(type = "items")` work on ECM fits.
* `summary()` returns the tables of a fit as one S3 object (`$fit`, `$items`, `$se`, `$parameters` with the prior of each parameter, ...), printed in the layout of tppcm's `irt_pars()` and blavaan; `as.data.frame()` gives the parameter table.
* `algorithm()` lists the computation steps of a fit: every full conditional of the Gibbs sampler in the order of one iteration (also the partially collapsed version), or the E- and M-steps, SQUAREM and standard errors of `ecm()`, with the prior constants substituted and the building block (`draw_*()`) of each step; Unicode or `format = "latex"`.
* `equations()` writes the model as equations (Unicode by default, `format = "latex"` for an `aligned` block), with the parameter symbols, the items they apply to and the priors.
* Models: `mlirt()`, `rtirt_null()`, `rtirt_latreg()`, `rtirt_latent()` and `rtirt_cross()`, each with quantile versions (`quantile =`).
* Data input with checks: `input_data(resp, time, cov, id, data)`.
* Sampling with `gibbs()`; `gibbs(init = "ecm")` starts the chains at the EM fit.
* `gibbs(collapse = TRUE)`: a partially collapsed sampler (van Dyk & Park, 2008). Speed is integrated out of the ability, RT item and structural regression updates (ability out of the `mlirt()` regression), and the 2PL item parameters (a, b) are drawn jointly with the Polya-Gamma variables integrated out (Metropolis-Hastings with a Fisher-scoring proposal in (a, -a b), followed by the usual Polya-Gamma steps). Same posterior; on TIMSS the smallest effective sample size grows 1.9-7 times (1.3-5 times per second); no gain for 1PL models or `rtirt_latreg(speed_var = "fixed")`. The default sampler is unchanged.
* `rtirt_priors()`: the prior distributions of `gibbs()` (and of the item parameters for `ecm(prior = "map")`) can be changed; the Prior column of `summary()` and `equations()` show the priors used. The defaults are common data-independent priors (a ~ N+(1, 1), b ~ N(0, 3^2), lambda ~ N(0, 10^2), half-t(3, 1) on the residual and speed SDs, N(0, 1) for regression coefficients, rho and c) and every full conditional stays conjugate: the half-t priors are sampled through the inverse-gamma auxiliary variable of Huang & Wand (2013). `rtirt_priors_julia()` gives the priors of ExtendedRtIrtModeling.jl (b truncated to [-4, 4], lambda centred at the mean and SD of the log times, IG(0.001, 0.001) variances) and reproduces the earlier draws exactly.
* `score_test()`: score-based tests of parameter invariance (Merkle & Zeileis, 2013) from one `ecm()` fit, with casewise scores from `estfun()` (and `bread()`) for 'strucchange' and 'partykit': DIF along gender, item position or the total response time (valid for the accuracy parameters in the joint model, with decorrelation within the tested block), per item with Holm adjustment, a message when the moderator is related to ability (impact), and cluster-level moderators.
* Building blocks (`?blocks`, `draw_*()`): the full conditionals of the samplers callable from R, so new samplers can be composed in R with every draw in C++; `inst/examples/ex07_blocks.R` rewrites the `rtirt_cross()` sampler with them and reproduces `gibbs()` to rounding. `draw_pg()` draws Pólya–Gamma variables for any matrix of logits, so models from the literature can be composed: `ex08_testlet.R` (Bradlow, Wainer & Wang, 1999), `ex09_mirt_rt.R` (compensatory multidimensional IRT with response times; Man, Harring, Jiao & Zhan, 2019) and `ex10_conditional_dependence.R` (Bolsinova, De Boeck & Tijmstra, 2017).
* `check_sampler()`: simulation checks that a sampler draws from the right posterior, for any model and setting of `gibbs()`: the joint distribution test of Geweke (2004; successive-conditional simulator compared with the prior through prior quantiles, ESS-based z-tests, Holm adjustment) and simulation-based calibration (Cook, Gelman & Rubin, 2006; Talts et al., 2018). `sampler_priors` checks the check (a wrong prior must be detected). `scripts/60_check_samplers.R` runs the Geweke test for all samplers.
* `person_fit()`: the RT person-fit statistic of Marianti et al. (2014), evaluated in every iteration of `gibbs()` (posterior mean and posterior probability of exceeding the chi-square 95% quantile; normal log times).
* `ci_test()`: Lagrange multiplier test of conditional independence between responses and response times (van der Linden & Glas, 2010; shift of the log times of correct responses), from an `ecm()` fit with the observed information of the extended model (correct size; the outer-product version rejected too often).
* Helpers for composing samplers (`?compose`): `calc_normal_part()` (precision and linear term of a parameter entering normal pseudo-observations: Pólya–Gamma, probit or log times), `draw_mvn()` (batched multivariate normal draws in precision form, with truncation), `draw_items_mh()` (item parameters with the latent responses integrated out, logit or probit), `draw_invwishart()`, `draw_mean_mvn()`, `draw_cor()`, `calc_person_fit()`. The examples ex09-ex12 are rewritten with them (same results, 1.5-2.5 times faster).
* Notation: persons j, items i (y_ij is the response of person j to item i) in `equations()`, `algorithm()`, the documentation and the tutorial, as in birt.
* Default item names are zero-padded (`item01`, ...).
* `preknowledge_test()`: statistics of Sinharay (2020) for item preknowledge (RT person fit chi_pf, signed likelihood ratio of speed Lambda_s and of ability L_s on a compromised item set). `inst/examples/ex13_preknowledge.R` reproduces Table 2 of Sinharay (2020) on the public licensure data, and `ex12_variable_speed.R` the variable-speed model of Fox & Marianti (2016) on the Amsterdam Chess data.
* `estimates()` / `convergence()` with `diagnostics = "rank"`: rank-normalized split R-hat and bulk/tail ESS (Vehtari et al., 2021; package posterior).
* `draw_probit_z()`: latent normal responses of Albert & Chib (1993) for probit models. `inst/examples/ex11_lnirt.R` composes the LNIRT model (probit, time discrimination, item-level multivariate normal) and reproduces Fox & Marianti (2017, Table 2) on the public credential data, with the latent responses integrated out for (a, b) (Fisher-scoring Metropolis-Hastings) against the slow mixing of easy items; about 17 times the minimum ESS per second of LNIRT.
* `ecm()`: maximum marginal likelihood for the normal models (Bock-Aitkin EM with Polya-Gamma item steps, SQUAREM, observed-information standard errors), with `logLik()`, `vcov()`, `AIC()` and `BIC()`. Without a latent regression all persons share the quadrature nodes and the E- and M-steps collapse to matrix products on the Bock-Aitkin artificial data (the same iterates; 5000 x 60: fit 10.5 -> 1.0 s, standard errors 143 -> 20 s); person-specific nodes use C++ kernels.
* Internal clean-up (results unchanged up to rounding): one formula for the normal integral over speed (`ecm()`, `marginal_loglik()`, `calc_normal_given_nodes()`), one 2PL log-likelihood at the nodes (`calc_loglik_nodes()`, with softplus: `dic()` and `marginal_loglik()` no longer overflow for very large a(theta - b)), Gauss-Hermite nodes only from `calc_nodes()`, `pg_mean()` in C++, one Fisher-scoring Metropolis-Hastings step behind `draw_items_joint()`, `draw_items_mh()` and `gibbs(collapse = TRUE)`. `runSimulation()` calls `run_simulation()` (new argument `speed_var`). `ecm(quadrature = "ba81", analytic = FALSE)` warns with the gap to adaptive quadrature and advises `analytic = TRUE`.
* EM components (`?em_components`): `calc_nodes()`, `calc_loglik_nodes()`, `calc_normal_given_nodes()`, `calc_node_weights()`, `calc_artificial_data()`, `calc_item_sums()`, `update_items_logistic()`, `em_squarem()` and `calc_hessian()`; `ecm()` is built from them and they compose new EM algorithms.
* Three estimation engines for declared models, sharing the model and the step list (Gibbs draws from each full conditional, ECM moves to its mode with the latent parts averaged, CAVI sets q to it with expected coefficients):
  * `ecm()`: ECM. The person parameters are integrated by adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature (frozen centres, recentred between rounds; all nodes stacked); a person parameter that enters only normal parts linearly (e.g. speed) is integrated exactly given the others; Polya-Gamma variables are replaced by E[omega]; `step_mala()` becomes gradient ascent (GEM); standard errors from Louis' formula (expected complete-data Hessian plus the covariance of the complete-data score, from the first and second derivatives of the declared expressions; `run_em(hessian =)`), equal to the numerical derivative of the Fisher-identity score to 1e-7 and about 10 times faster. `quadrature = "ba81"`: Bock & Aitkin (1981): the same nodes for every person; data parts that depend on the persons only through the grid variables are collapsed to artificial data per node and item (E-step by matrix products, M-step on nodes x items); still ECM. The normal parts of a step are summed in C++ (`.ml_part`). When every part of an exactly integrated variable is linear in all latent variables with a variance free of them (speed in the cross and hierarchical RT models), the exact step is computed from sums over items once per person (p, and h linear in the grid variables) instead of at every node, and the M-step of those parts uses each person's posterior mean and covariance of the latent variables through 2L points (exact, since the parts are quadratic in them); otherwise the general path is used. The declared cross model reproduces `ecm(rtirt_cross())` (log-likelihood equal, estimates within 1e-4, same standard errors) in 0.25 s for 300 x 8 and 2.1 s (`quadrature = "ba81"`: 1.7 s) for 1000 x 20 (15.4 s before, with numerical standard errors).
  * `vi()`: CAVI with q(person) free-form on the same grid, truncated-normal q for normal priors, inverse-gamma q for variances (half-t through the Huang-Wand auxiliary) and q(omega | theta) = PG(1, c) (Durante & Rigon, 2019); exact mean-field expectations from the multilinear form of the expressions; the ELBO never decreases. Means close to the posterior; SDs of item parameters too small, as for any mean-field approximation.
  * `ecm(method = "newton")` or `"quasi-newton"`: maximize the same objective directly with `nlminb()` within the parameter bounds (gradient by Fisher's identity, Hessian by Louis' formula; Glas & van der Linden, 2010); no closed-form steps needed. Same maximum as ECM; ECM is faster on large models (1000 x 20 RT model: 2.2 s vs 12.9 s), Newton is faster when a parameter has no closed-form step (2PL with exp(la): 2.9 s vs 0.4 s).
  * Fixes from an independent review of the blocks layer: a truncated prior whose mean or sd uses a parameter is refused (its normalizing constant would depend on the parameter); observed data given as person vectors work in `ecm()` and `vi()` (masks stacked; the C++ kernels check every length); with `prior = "ml"` the priors of fixed parameters are left out of every part (score, M-step, Hessian) and a parameter that appears only in such priors is refused; parameters at a bound of their prior get no standard error, with a warning; `quadrature = "ba81"` compares its objective with adaptive nodes at the estimates (`fit$adaptive_gap`) and warns when the common nodes are too coarse. `check_vi()` also checks the scales of the variance factors of q.
  * `equations()` of a `block_model()` and `algorithm()` of a `vi()` fit: Unicode (default) or `format = "latex"` (an `aligned` block, as `equations()` of the built-in models); indices j (person) and i (item) added. The CAVI algorithm is written the mean-field way: the model p as a product of its parts, the factorized q, the update rule log q*(x) = E_-x[log p] + const (Bishop, 2006, Eq. 10.9), and the form of each factor of q with its expected coefficients.
  * `check_ecm()` and `check_vi()`: the counterparts of the Geweke check (every step raises the objective from many starts; Fisher identity; the fit is a maximum). `check_ecm()` also checks hand-written EMs and the EM of `ecm()` for the built-in normal models (`check_ecm(rtirt_cross(data))`).
  * `step_gibbs(..., cut = "logT")`: a cut (Plummer, 2015).
  * Definitions as in `rethinking::ulam()`: `eta <- a * (theta - b)`.
  * Expressions are compiled to a multilinear form (sum of monomials) and evaluated in C++ without expanding person and item vectors to matrices.
* Declared models for the building blocks, in the style of PyMC: `block_model()` declares the model once (`theta[person] ~ normal(0, 1)`, `Y ~ bernoulli_logit(a * (theta - b))`, variances with `half_t()` or `inv_gamma()`), and `gibbs()` runs stacked steps that only name parameters: `step_gibbs()` (normal full conditionals summed over every part of the model that uses the parameter; conjugate variances), `step_mala()` (exact likelihood; log scale with the Jacobian for bounded parameters; adapted step sizes) and `step_shift()` (generalized Gibbs translations, e.g. `step_shift(rho = -1, zeta = theta)`). The Polya-Gamma variables are hidden and redrawn when a step needs them (once per iteration and after MALA moved a parameter of the logistic part), so stale-omega errors cannot occur; steps that do not cover every parameter, mix dimensions, or ask Gibbs for a nonlinear parameter are refused with a hint. `algorithm()` and `equations()` describe the composed sampler and the model; `check_sampler(model, steps = )` runs the Geweke test with parameters and data simulated from the declaration. Checks: the declared cross model reproduces `gibbs(rtirt_cross())` (41 parameters, max 2.0 Monte Carlo SEs); 58 random step stacks for a 2PL all match a long reference (hand-written steps: 23 of 240 random stacks gave a wrong posterior).
* A workflow layer for the building blocks, after a usability test with a graduate-student reader (samplers and EM written as "a state list + one step function"):
  * `run_sampler(data, init, step, monitor)`: chains, burn-in, thinning, storage, running person means and the summary table (mean, SD, interval, R-hat, ESS); `n_iter`, `n_burnin`, `n_thin` and `n_chain` as in `gibbs()`.
  * Normal parts: `irt_part()` and `calc_normal_part()` return a normal part (precision and linear term); `normal_prior(mean, sd)` adds a prior with `+`, and `draw(part, lower, upper)` draws (truncated when bounds are given). No hand conversion of priors to the precision form.
  * `check_sampler(step = , prior = , simulate = , monitor = )`: Geweke's joint distribution test for samplers written by the user (rejects a wrong prior).
  * `draw_shift()`: exact shift along directions identified only by the priors (e.g. the intercept of a latent regression with the difficulties).
  * `run_em(data, init, step, loglik, positive)`: packing, log scale for positive parameters, SQUAREM, convergence and standard errors from the Hessian of `loglik()`; `estep_irt()` and `mstep_items()` are the E- and M-steps of a 2PL (person-specific prior means, missing responses, ML or MAP). A 2PL written this way equals mirt's estimates and standard errors.
  * Readable errors: dimension checks in `draw_omega()` (argument order), `irt_part()`, `draw_items_pg()`, `draw_items_joint()`, `calc_normal_part()`; `draw_gauss()` refuses non-positive precisions; `draw_mala()` checks `logpost()` and takes `joint = TRUE` for one target (a scalar `lp` with several elements used to be applied elementwise).
  * Missing responses (NA) are skipped by `irt_part()`, `draw_items_pg()` and `draw_items_joint()`.
  * `reg_part(X, y, w)`: the normal part of regression coefficients (a precision matrix), `draw(reg_part(X, theta) + normal_prior(0, 10))`.
  * `b_mean` in `draw_items_pg()`, `draw_items_joint()` and `mstep_items()`: a prior mean of b per item (LLTM with item covariates). `mstep_items()` takes the item priors as `priors = rtirt_priors()`, as the samplers do.
  * `print()` of a `block_fit` warns when R-hat > 1.05 or ESS < 100 and names the usual cures; `run_em()` warns when the objective decreases, when `s$.loglik` differs from `loglik()`, when the estimates are not a maximum of `loglik()` (a wrong M-step converges to the wrong values; also when the Hessian is not negative definite), and when `step()` changes no parameter (no M-step); SQUAREM stops on non-finite steps. `draw_sigma2()` and `draw_lambda()` stop with a message when the log times contain NA (they returned NaN before); use `calc_normal_part()` with `draw()` and `draw_var()` for missing times.
  * `draw_items_joint()` and `draw_items_mh()` return `list(a, b)` with the attribute `accepted`, as `draw_mala()` and `draw_cor()`.
  * Help pages are written with roxygen markdown (code, lists, tables and `%` render correctly).
  * The blocks tutorial: a JAGS-to-blocks table, the Pólya–Gamma idea before the first example, `check_sampler(step = )` (and what it cannot detect), identification and `draw_shift()` (with the shift-invariant quantities to report), an LLTM with `b_mean` and `reg_part()`, and a table of common mistakes, and writing an EM with `run_em()` (with the warnings for a wrong M-step).
* `draw_mala()` and `mcmc_adapt()` (`?mala`): vectorized Metropolis-adjusted Langevin steps with Robbins-Monro step adaptation, for blocks without a conjugate full conditional; `draw_cor(method = "mala")` (about 2.5-3 times the ESS of the random walk; the default stays `"rw"`).
* `marginal_loglik()` and marginal DIC / WAIC / PSIS-LOO in `fit_indices()`.
* The generics shared with birt dispatch to both packages whichever was attached last.
* Two tutorials (`inst/tutorial/`): `birtRcpp_intro` (the ready-made models, for beginners) and `birtRcpp_blocks` (samplers and EM from the building blocks), each code chunk explained line by line.
* `convergence()`: the share of converged parameters was NaN without tail ESS (classic diagnostics); the R-hat threshold is 1.05, as in `print()`.
* Accessors: `summary()`, `estimates()`, `scores()`, `reliability()`, `fit_indices()` and `convergence()`. `summary()` lists every parameter with its prior (as blavaan). The person variables are named `ability` and `speed` in scores, reliabilities and parameter names (`beta_ability`, `b_ability`, `cor_ability_speed`).
* Julia-style aliases.
